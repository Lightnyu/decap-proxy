OUR RESEARCH PRIORITIES

Questions worth investigating.
Evidence worth earning.

Our programme explores whether naturally derived compounds can provide useful starting points for future therapeutic research. Each area begins with testable questions, controlled methods and independent validation.

01 / ONCOLOGY

Oncology research

Investigating selected truffle-derived compounds and their biological activity in relevant laboratory models. The aim is to identify reproducible signals that may warrant further study—not to claim a cancer treatment.

02 / ANTIVIRAL

Antiviral discovery

Screening and characterising compounds against carefully selected viral research models, with attention to assay validity, selectivity, mechanism and independent replication.

03 / ANTIBACTERIAL

Antibacterial discovery

Exploring activity against selected bacterial models and resistance-related questions, alongside selectivity and safety work needed to judge whether a finding merits progression.

04 / NUTRACEUTICAL COMPOUNDS

Characterisation before translation

Studying composition, stability, bioavailability and safety questions around truffle-derived compounds that may have a future nutraceutical application. Any product pathway would depend on evidence, quality controls and the applicable food and claims regulations.

Research interest · not a health claim
FACILITIES & CAPABILITY

Building the tools to ask better questions.

We are growing our research and development facilities across the UK. Our capability plan brings together extraction, analytical chemistry, biological screening and data systems, with specialist university and laboratory partners where appropriate.

The goal is a connected, reproducible workflow: know what is in a sample, test it carefully, document what happened and determine whether the evidence supports the next step.

Equipment is a means to evidence.

AGRICULTURE · PLACE · PARTNERSHIP

More than a culinary masterpiece.

Our truffle orchards and plantations connect research with the living systems that make cultivation possible. We are expanding this work and building relationships with UK growers to develop long-term, carefully managed growing environments.

Landscape is part of the research context: soil, climate, host trees and mycorrhizal relationships shape cultivation and the biological material available for study. These factors must be documented and controlled where possible; they do not, by themselves, demonstrate a medical effect.

From a natural sample to a medicine:
many evidence gates in between.

Therapeutic development is staged. A promising laboratory result is a beginning, not proof of benefit. Each step must meet scientific, safety, ethical and regulatory requirements before progression.
